How should M and S be arranged?

Each approach uses 2 cells (heatmap + slice). The question is whether M and S share a figure or live in separate cells.

📓 Cell 1 — Strike price S
Heatmap S [EUR/MWh]
Slice A_G=0.5
📓 Cell 2 — Contract volume M
Heatmap M [GWh/yr]
Slice A_G=0.5

A — Separate cells per variable

Each variable (S, M) gets its own notebook cell: heatmap on the left, A_G=0.5 slice on the right as subplots. Clean, self-contained, easy to cite individually.

Pros

  • Each cell is independently readable
  • No scale-mismatch confusion
  • Easy to reuse in paper as separate figures

Cons

  • 2 cells → 2 figures → more notebook clutter
📓 Cell 1 — S and M (combined 2×2)
Heatmap S
Slice S (A_G=0.5)
Heatmap M
Slice M (A_G=0.5)

B — One cell, 2×2 combined figure

Single cell with a 2×2 subplot grid: top row = S (heatmap + slice), bottom row = M (heatmap + slice). One plt.savefig → one figure file.

Pros

  • One figure compares S and M side-by-side
  • Compact notebook

Cons

  • Subplots smaller — harder to read heatmap cells
  • Less flexible for paper layout
